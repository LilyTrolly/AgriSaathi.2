Biodegradable soil-improvement materials

Compost

Adds organic matter and can improve soil structure.

Farmyard manure

Well-decomposed manure can add nutrients and organic matter.

Green manure

Suitable crops such as dhaincha can be grown and incorporated into soil.

Crop-residue mulch

Clean crop residues can help protect soil and reduce moisture loss.

Vermicompost

A source of organic matter and plant nutrients.

Neem cake

An organic amendment sometimes used in crop and soil management.

Use materials that are mature, clean and suitable for your crop. Test soil first and follow local agricultural advice before changing fertiliser rates or applying amendments.

🗺️ India Soil & Crop Health Guide

An introductory guide to major Indian soil groups and common soil-related and plant-health problems.

Alluvial soil

Common in river plains, including parts of Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and West Bengal. Often supports rice, wheat and other crops, depending on water and nutrients.

Black soil

Found across parts of Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and the Deccan region. Often associated with cotton; can become sticky when wet.

Red soil

Occurs in parts of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. Organic matter and nutrient levels vary by location.

Laterite soil

Found in parts of the Western Ghats, Kerala, Karnataka and the eastern hills. Can be acidic and nutrient-poor in some areas.

Arid soil

Common in Rajasthan and other dry regions. Low rainfall and salt accumulation can be challenges.

Forest and mountain soil

Found in Himalayan and hill regions. Properties vary with altitude, slope and vegetation.

Common soil and plant-health problems

Soil salinity

Excess soluble salts can make it harder for plants to absorb water. Soil and irrigation-water testing helps identify the issue.

Soil erosion

Wind and water can remove fertile topsoil. Cover crops, mulch and contour-based practices may help.

Root rot

Some fungi and fungus-like organisms cause root diseases, often encouraged by poor drainage.

Wilt diseases

Some soil-borne pathogens can cause wilting in crops. Diagnosis is crop-specific.

Nutrient deficiency

Low nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium or micronutrients can affect growth. Soil testing helps guide treatment.

Plant pests and diseases

Insects, viruses, bacteria and fungi affect different crops. Correct identification matters before treatment.

Important: Soil itself does not have a single list of "diseases". Soil-borne pathogens can cause plant diseases. Do not diagnose a crop problem from soil type alone.
